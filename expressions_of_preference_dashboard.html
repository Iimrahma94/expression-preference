01 · UNDERSTAND

What are Expressions of Preference?

Expressions of Preference are phrases used to say which person, thing, activity, or option we like or choose more than another.

Example: I prefer tea to coffee.
Artinya: Saya lebih suka teh daripada kopi.

We use these expressions to talk about personal choices, compare alternatives, ask what other people prefer, and explain our reasons.

Expressions & Grammar Patterns

Study the formula first, then read the example aloud.

02 · LANGUAGE FOCUS

1. Prefer + noun

Used to compare two things.

Subject + prefer(s) + A + to + B
I prefer tea to coffee.
Saya lebih suka teh daripada kopi.

For he/she/it in the simple present, use prefers.

2. Prefer + V-ing

Used to compare two activities.

Subject + prefer(s) + V-ing + to + V-ing
I prefer reading to watching TV.
Saya lebih suka membaca daripada menonton TV.

3. Prefer + to-infinitive

Used to state a general preference.

Subject + prefer(s) + to + V1
I prefer to study at night.
Saya lebih suka belajar pada malam hari.

4. Would prefer

Often used for a particular or current choice.

Subject + would prefer + to + V1
I would prefer to work in Japan.
Saya lebih memilih bekerja di Jepang.

5. Would rather

Used to say which action you would choose.

Subject + would rather + V1 + than + V1
I would rather stay than leave.
Saya lebih memilih tinggal daripada pergi.

6. Like ... better than

A common expression in everyday conversation.

Subject + like(s) + A + better than + B
She likes English better than Math.
Dia lebih menyukai bahasa Inggris daripada Matematika.

Quick grammar reminder

Remember these common patterns.

Prefer A TO B · Prefer doing A TO doing BUse to to compare nouns or -ing activities after prefer.
Would rather + V1 + THAN + V1Do not add “to” after would rather. Say “I would rather stay,” not “I would rather to stay.”
Would prefer + TO + V1Say “I would prefer to work,” not “I would prefer work.”

Examples & Dialogues

03 · SEE IT IN CONTEXT

Dialogue 1 · Choosing a drink

A: Would you prefer tea or coffee?
B: I would prefer tea, please.
A: Why?
B: Because tea helps me relax. What about you?
A: I prefer coffee to tea because I like its strong taste.

Focus: “would prefer” and “prefer A to B”.

Dialogue 2 · Study habits

A: Do you prefer studying alone or with friends?
B: I prefer studying with friends because we can discuss difficult questions.
A: I see. I would rather study alone than study in a group.
B: Why is that?
A: Because I can concentrate better when I study alone.

Focus: compare activities and give reasons.

Dialogue 3 · Choosing a career

A: What kind of job would you prefer after graduation?
B: I would prefer to work in a manufacturing company.
A: Why do you prefer that?
B: Because I want to improve my technical skills and gain experience.
A: I see. I would rather work in an international company than work in a small company.
B: That's interesting! We have different career goals.

Focus: workplace vocabulary and explaining a career preference.
